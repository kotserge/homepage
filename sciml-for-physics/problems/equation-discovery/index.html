Under equation discovery, we try to identify the governing equation itself from dense measurements of the state. In the most general case, we have no insight into the operator itself, beyond an assumption about the hypothesis class F spanning it, and we aim for the simplest operator in it that reproduces the measured dynamics. More formally, what we seek is

N⋆=argN∈Fmin​ ​∂t​u−N(u)​2+αC(N),

where u are the state measurements in space and time, ∂t​u is their time derivative, and C is the operator's complexity, weighted by some α. Most commonly, F is a library of candidate terms, where one searches a sparse combination of

N(u)=Θ(u)ξ,Θ(u)=[1, u, u2, ux​, uux​, uxx​, …],C(N)=∥ξ∥0​,

with ξ the vector of coefficients and ∥ξ∥0​ its number of nonzero entries. The goal is to find the support of ξ and its values, i.e., the equation.
