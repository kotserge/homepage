In reduced-order modeling, we replace a large discretized system by a low-dimensional latent representation in which the solution can be simulated cheaply. It thereby approximates the same parameter-to-solution map as operator learning, but through a compressed state rather than directly.

More formally, let u(t;λ)∈RNh​ be the full discretized state for parameters λ, drawn from a measure μ on the parameter range Λ⊂Rp of interest. We seek a decoder D:Rr→RNh​, an encoder E:RNh​→Rr and reduced dynamics g such that

D,E,gmin​ Eλ∼μ​∫0T​​u(t;λ)−D(z(t;λ))​2dts.t.z˙=g(z;λ),z(0)=E(u(0;λ)),

with z∈Rr and r≪Nh​. The initial state is encoded once, z is integrated entirely in the latent space, and the full state is decoded only when needed. Classically, D(z)=Vz and E(u)=V⊤u with an orthonormal basis V∈RNh​×r obtained by POD from snapshots, and g follows by projecting the full-order dynamics u˙=F(u;λ), i.e., g(z;λ)=V⊤F(Vz;λ). Alternatively, machine-learning techniques such as autoencoders can learn a nonlinear low-dimensional representation, with g fitted to the projected snapshots.
