Model discrepancy captures the gap between a simulator and the real system for which it stands in. Most problems use a large corpus originating from a simulator Nsim​, while for real measurements Nreal​=Nsim​+δ, only a few samples are available. Estimating this δ under such settings becomes the target

p(λ,δ∣y) ∝ p(y∣usim,λ​+δ)p(λ)p(δ),

where λ are the coefficients, usim,λ​ the solution of the simulator under them, δ the discrepancy to reality, and y=H(ureal​)+ϵ the real, sparse measurements with some noise ϵ. The priors p(λ) and p(δ) are part of the problem, since λ and δ are confounded: a wrong coefficient and a missing term can explain the same measurements.

01

Nearby problems

Closure & Hybrid Modeling. Also learns a missing term, but from a finer simulation, i.e., with plenty of data from the operator it targets. Here the missing term has to be learned from reality, with far less data.

Inverse Problem. Assumes the model is exact, so that any misfit is attributed to λ and the noise. With δ=0, this attribution biases the recovered coefficients.

Policy Learning. Trains in a simulator and acts in the experiment, so the same gap enters through the environment rather than through the measurements.
