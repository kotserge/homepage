The inverse problem is (most commonly) associated with the recovery of the coefficients of a known equation from indirect, noisy observations of its solution. Hence, the aim is to find λ such that the solution uλ​ explains the observations. Under an observational model:

y=H(u)+εwithε∼N(0,Γ),

where N(0,Γ) is a Gaussian noise source with covariance Γ. Then we aim to find

λ⋆=argλmin​ ∥y−H(uλ​)∥Γ−12​+R(λ)

where λ are the coefficients, uλ​ the solution of the forward problem under λ (calculated by some surrogate or exactly), y the observations with operator H, and R a regularization term. This can also be learned similarly in Bayesian form:

p(λ∣y)∝p(y∣λ)p(λ),

with p(λ) being the prior, p(y∣λ) the likelihood of the observations under the forward map (and noise), and p(λ∣y) the posterior. The coefficient can represent scalars, vectors, but also functions, which (can) make the problem ill-posed, as the problem becomes not surjective. This is where the reguralization term replaces the problem with a well-posed one (restoring uniqueness and stability).

01

Nearby problems

Forward Problem. The inverse to the Inverse Problem. Assuming all information is given, learn the solution uλ​.

Data Assimilation. Starts from the same observation model, but λ is known and the initial state u0​ is sought instead.

Joint State & Parameter Estimation. Seeks the initial state u0​ together with the coefficients λ, rather than λ alone.

Equation Discovery. Drops the known form of N, so that the operator itself, not only its coefficients, is sought.

Model Discrepancy & Sim-To-Real. Drops the assumption that the model is exact, so that a missing term δ competes with λ in explaining the observations.

Control & Design. Also optimizes an input of the system, but for an objective J rather than to explain observations, so there is no true value to recover.

02

Methods possible

Physics-Informed Neural Networks. Physics-Informed Neural Networks can be extended by a parameter, representing the coefficient of the system. Therefore, the coefficient becomes an additional learnable parameter and can be jointly optimized with the parameters of the model.
