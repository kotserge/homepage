The inverse problem is (most commonly) associated with the recovery of the coefficients of a known equation from indirect, noisy observations of its solution. Hence, what one aims is to map from its λ such that the solution u(λ) explains the observations.

λ⋆=argλ​min ∥y−H(u(λ))∥Γ−12​+R(λ),

where λ are the coefficients, u(λ) the solution of the forward problem for them, y the observations with operator H and noise covariance Γ, and R a regulariser (makes the problem "well"-posed). Given are the form of N with f, b and u0​, a forward map λ↦u(λ) (exact or a surrogate), and the observations. Sought is λ: scalars, or fields such as a conductivity or a permeability. The state is reconstructed on the way, not sought.

p(λ∣y)∝p(y∣λ)p(λ)

The same problem in Bayesian form. p(λ) is the prior, p(y∣λ) the likelihood of the observations under the forward map and the noise model, and p(λ∣y) the posterior, which is sought as a whole rather than as one point. The first formulation is its maximum a posteriori point when R=−logp(λ).

A learning formulation

λ^∈argλmin​ 21​​H(u(λ))−y​Γ−12​+R(λ)

With R=−logp(λ) this is the MAP estimate of p(λ∣y)∝p(y∣λ)p(λ).

data
The observations y of this instance, with their noise covariance Γ. No corpus; the prior p(λ) or the regulariser stands in for one.
model
The forward map λ↦u(λ), exact or a surrogate; the unknown is λ. The PINN variant adds a network uθ​ and treats λ as extra parameters; diffusion priors add a generative model over λ(x).
optimization
Gradient-based with the adjoint or a differentiable solver; Gauss–Newton when the forward map is cheap. For the posterior: MCMC on a surrogate, or guided diffusion sampling.
physics
Model, as the forward map; Loss, as the residual in the PINN variant.
02

Insight

The regulariser is a prior in disguise: ill-posed problems need one even for a point estimate. Sparse data leaves directions of λ that no amount of optimisation can fix, and a surrogate inside the loop will be exploited where it is wrong.
