In emulation and time stepping, the target is to learn a surrogate from trajectories that is able to advance the state of a system in time. In contrast to the classical forward problem, where the system is fully specified, here that information is only implicit in the data: from solved trajectories alone we learn a time-stepper that maps the current state to the next one. More formally, we seek

Φ⋆=argΦmin​Eut:t+m​​j=1∑m​​Φj(ut​)−ut+j​​2,

where Φ is a learned one-step map approximating M, Φj=Φ∘⋯∘Φ its j-fold composition (a rollout over j steps), and the expectation runs over trajectory segments ut:t+m​ of a single system with λ, f and b fixed. Importantly, the rollout over the horizon m is part of the objective, since most applications require stability over many steps (compare to classical discretization methods). For chaotic systems in particular, a pointwise error over long horizons rewards blurring towards the mean, so the statistics of the rollout (e.g., invariant measure, spectra) become the target instead.

Nearby problems

Forward Problem. In contrast to learning an emulator from data, we have full knowledge and learn the solution to the fixed instance of a problem.

Parametric Emulation. The generalization of this problem towards a family of systems, where the coefficients of the operator varies across trajectories.

Foundation Emulators. The generalization of this problem towards learning a stepper over many dynamics/systems instead of a single fixed system.

Dynamics from Partial Observations. Learns the same one-step map, but from sparse, noisy observations instead of full trajectories.
