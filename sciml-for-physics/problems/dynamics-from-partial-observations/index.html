Dynamics from partial observations encompasses the joint learning of a system's dynamics and state when it is only partially, and possibly noisily, observed. In this sense, nothing about the operator, its coefficients, or the forcing is known; they are absorbed into Φθ​, a learned approximation of the one-step map M, which, together with the trajectory, must be consistent with the observations. Hence, we seek

(θ⋆,u0:T⋆​)=argθ, u0:T​min​ t=1∑T​​yt​−H(ut​)​Γ−12​+t=0∑T−1​​ut+1​−Φθ​(ut​)​Q−12​,

with Φθ​ as the one-step model, u0:T​ the trajectory, yt​ the observations under the observation model H with some noise covariance Γ, i.e., εt​∼N(0,Γ), and Q as the model-error covariance, i.e., ut+1​=Φθ​(ut​)+ηt​,ηt​∼N(0,Q). Therefore, the first term ties the trajectory to the data, and the second ties it to the model.

Data Assimilation. Unlike data assimilation, the model here is learned rather than given. Furthermore, the whole trajectory is unknown and must be estimated, whereas in data assimilation the estimation target is u0​.
