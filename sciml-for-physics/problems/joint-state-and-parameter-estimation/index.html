Joint state and parameter estimation is the generalization of Data Assimilation with additional unknown coefficients, i.e., the initial state as well as the coefficients of a known model are inferred from sparse, noisy observations over a window of the state. Hence, we seeks the following posterior of the state

p(u0​,λ∣y1:T​) ∝ p(u0​)p(λ)t=1∏T​p(yt​∣ut​),ut​=Mt​(u0​;λ),

with priors p(u0​) and p(λ), Mt​(⋅;λ) the model that carries the state forward to time t under the joint estimated coefficients λ, and yt​ of an observation model. The pair (u0​,λ) determines the whole trajectory, so every observation in the window constrains both. However, λ and u0​ might compensate each other in the fit, under which the observations may constrain only a combination of them. This is what makes the joint problem harder than either in vacuum.

01

Nearby problems

Data Assimilation. The special case where λ is known and only u0​ is sought. Conversely, appending the coefficients to the state, u~=(u,λ) with λ constant in time, turns the joint problem into data assimilation on u~.

Inverse Problem. The special case where the state is not an unknown of its own, so that only the coefficients λ have to explain the observations.

Dynamics from Partial Observations. Replaces the coefficients of a known model by the model itself, so that the dynamics, not only their parameters, are learned alongside the state.
