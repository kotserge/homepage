Data assimilation estimates a system's state at the start of a window from sparse, noisy observations, using a known model of its dynamics. Under an observational model yk​=H(uk​)+ϵk​ with Gaussian noise of covariance Γ, one seeks the posterior

p(u0​∣y1:K​) ∝ p(u0​)k=1∏K​p(yk​∣uk​),uk​=Mk​(u0​),

with prior p(u0​), Mk​ the model that carries the state forward to time k, and yk​ the observations under H. The targeted unknown is the state u0​ at the start of an assimilation window, and we estimate it from the observations in that window and a prior, so the trajectory it generates is consistent with both. Since the trajectory follows from this initial state, observations in the window constrain it, allowing us to determine the full field from these sparse observations.

01

Nearby problems

Inverse Problem. While the Inverse Problem starts from the same observational model, it seeks the coefficients λ that explain the observations, whereas here λ is known and the state u0​ is sought.

Dynamics from Partial Observations. The model M becomes an additional unknown alongside the (initial window) state. The windowed observations then have to constrain both the state and the dynamics.

Super-Resolution & Downscaling. In super-resolution the observational model becomes a coarse view on the problem with just one observation, from which the original state explaining this observation is seeked.
