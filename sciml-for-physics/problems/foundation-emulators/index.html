Foundation emulators seek to generalize across many systems and their dynamics (i.e., over N, λ, f, …) to allow for predictions in unobserved systems and in new families of systems. Given a short window of states of a new target system, such emulators infer its dynamics (implicitly N, λ, f, …) and predict the subsequent states, in either zero-shot (i.e., direct application) settings or by further adapting it to the new problem space (e.g., via fine-tuning). More formally, we seek

Φ⋆=argΦmin​ Es∼w​Eu∼μs​​Et​​Φ(ut−k:t​)−ut+1:t+m​​2,

where Φ is the emulator, s a family drawn from the pretraining mixture with weights w, μs​ its distribution of trajectories (over many N, λ, f, initial conditions, …), t a window position along the trajectory, ut−k:t​ the window of k+1 states the model conditions on, and ut+1:t+m​ the states to predict. In practice, Φ is often trained with m=1 and applied autoregressively to roll out longer horizons. However, similar to emulation & time stepping and parametric emulation, the leading window must be large enough to infer the dynamics of the system in question.

Nearby problems

In-Context Operator Learning. Instead of conditioning on the leading window, in-context operator learning conditions on demonstrations of the new operator, i.e., multiple examples of the system.

Parametric Emulation. The special case, where an emulator is searched for exactly one family.

Emulation & Time Stepping. The same task for one specific system.
