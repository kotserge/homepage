In parametric emulation, a surrogate is learned for a family of systems, which can advance the state of any instance of the family in time. In comparison to emulation & time stepping, where the coefficients are fixed and implicit in the data, they now vary across trajectories. Hence, we seek

Φ⋆=argΦmin​ Eλ∼μ​Eut:t+m​∣λ​j=1∑m​​Φj(ut​)−ut+j​​2,

where Φ is the time-stepper, μ the measure on the parameter range of interest, ut​,…,ut+m​ consecutive states of a trajectory with coefficients λ, and Φj the j-fold rollout with λ held fixed. Depending on what information is available about the parameters of the system, Φ needs to implicitly identify these from either a given history window ut−k:t​ or could be explicitly conditioned on λ and possibly the forcing f or the boundary data b. However, an identification from a history window requires that these parameters are actually identifiable from k steps. Slow or weakly observable parameters may require long windows, and parameters that do not affect the observed dynamics at all cannot be recovered.

Nearby problems

Emulation & Time Stepping. The special case of a single member of the family, where λ is fixed.

Parametric Operator Learning. Learns the map from the same kind of input to the solution in one shot, rather than as a step that is rolled out over a horizon.

Foundation Emulators. Drops λ from the input and widens the family to many, so that the coefficients have to be inferred from a window of states instead of being given.
