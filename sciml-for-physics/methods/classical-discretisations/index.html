Classical discretisations

Finite elements, finite volumes and spectral methods replace the continuous problem by a finite one and solve it exactly up to discretisation error. Nothing is learned; the equations are the model and the solver drives their residual to zero.

a(uh​,vh​)=ℓ(vh​)  ∀vh​∈Vh​orNh​[uh​;λ]=fh​

Vh​ is a finite-dimensional space of functions on a mesh, uh​∈Vh​ the discrete solution, a and ℓ the bilinear and linear forms of the weak formulation, and Nh​, fh​ the operator and forcing after discretisation. The method requires the operator explicitly, all coefficients, the forcing, the boundary data and the initial state; it uses no data. Accuracy is set by the mesh and the order, cost by the size of the linear or nonlinear systems.

Data
None; collocation or quadrature points of the mesh.
Model
A fixed basis of local or global functions; the equations hold in weak or strong form.
Optimization
Linear solvers, Newton iterations, time steppers; preconditioning decides the cost.

Where the physics enters The model, exactly; the residual is the loss.
