Physics-informed neural networks

A PINN represents the solution by a network of the coordinates and trains it on the residual of the equations, evaluated by automatic differentiation at sampled points. No solved data are needed; the equations, the boundary data and the initial state are the whole supervision. With a few observations and the coefficients left free, the same loss solves an inverse problem.

θmin​ ​N[uθ​;λ]−f​Ω×(0,T]2​+βb​​B[uθ​]−b​∂Ω2​+β0​​uθ​(⋅,0)−u0​​2

uθ​ is the network, θ its weights, the norms are Monte Carlo sums over collocation points in the interior, on the boundary and at t=0, and βb​, β0​ weight the terms. The method requires the operator explicitly, with its coefficients and forcing, and the initial state. Its difficulty is the conditioning of the residual, not a lack of data.

Data
Collocation points, resampled; no solutions.
Model
An MLP on (x,t) with smooth activations; hard constraints can build in boundary conditions.
Optimization
Adam then L-BFGS; second-order or curvature-aware methods for stiff problems.

Where the physics enters The loss, as the residual; the model, when constraints are built in.
